LitDataMatcher schema vocabulary review
=======================================

Purpose
-------
This note flags project-specific vocabulary that may shape how reviewers,
collaborators, or future annotators interpret the system. These are not standard
biomedical terms unless explicitly noted; they are local labels used to keep the
pipeline inspectable.

Renamed in this pass
--------------------
extraction_type -> question_origin
  Reason: "question_origin" better describes where the candidate question came
  from: an explicit question, a future-direction statement, a limitation-derived
  gap, or an unspecified source.

confidence -> extraction_confidence
  Scope: renamed only for literature extraction and evidence spans.
  Reason: plain "confidence" was too broad and could be confused with model
  certainty, reviewer confidence, dataset quality, or statistical confidence.

answerability_hint -> answerability
  Reason: the shorter term is clearer. It should still be read as a heuristic
  project score, not proof that a question is answerable.

Terms that may deserve future review
------------------------------------
expert_question_quality
  Current meaning: human-entered review score for question quality.
  Potential issue: "expert" may imply a specialist has already reviewed every
  row. Consider "question_quality_score" or "review_question_quality".

expert_data_match_quality
  Current meaning: human-entered review score for how well a dataset matches a
  question.
  Potential issue: same "expert" ambiguity. Consider "data_match_quality_score"
  or "review_data_match_quality".

expert_notes
  Current meaning: free-text notes from a reviewer.
  Potential issue: could simply be "review_notes".

QuestionLabel.label
  Current meaning: broad annotation decision field.
  Potential issue: "label" is too generic. Consider "question_decision" or
  "question_label_type" once annotation guidelines are written.

quality_score
  Current meaning: coarse dataset metadata-quality score.
  Potential issue: broad phrase. Consider "dataset_quality_score" if the field
  becomes user-facing or appears in tables.

semantic_relevance
  Current meaning: lexical/semantic similarity component in ranking.
  Potential issue: current implementation is mostly deterministic lexical
  fallback logic. Avoid implying deep semantic understanding until embedding or
  model-based matching is added and validated.

feasibility
  Current meaning: whether dataset metadata suggests a plausible analysis path.
  Potential issue: broad. In narrative text, prefer "analysis feasibility" or
  "match feasibility".

governance
  Current meaning: access, license, reuse, and human-subject risk context.
  Potential issue: acceptable if defined, but broad in isolation. In tables,
  "reuse_governance" may read more clearly.

uncertainty_penalty
  Current meaning: ranking penalty from weak or uncertain literature synthesis.
  Potential issue: not statistical uncertainty. Keep docs clear that this is a
  ranking heuristic.

novelty_score and significance_score
  Current meaning: deterministic heuristic scores used for triage.
  Potential issue: these can sound like validated scientific claims. Public
  documentation should call them project-derived triage scores until benchmarked.

capability_type
  Current meaning: class of direct or derived dataset capability.
  Potential issue: needs a controlled vocabulary before large annotation work.

DerivedVariableRule
  Current meaning: a cataloged possibility that a variable could be derived from
  available fields.
  Potential issue: readers may assume calculations are performed. Documentation
  should keep saying this records likely derivability, not computed results.

Recommended naming posture
--------------------------
Use concrete names when possible:
  - "extraction_confidence" instead of "confidence" for extractor certainty.
  - "match_relevance" instead of "expert_relevance" for pair-level relevance.
  - "question_origin" instead of "extraction_type" for candidate genesis.
  - "answerability" only with caveats that it is a heuristic score.

Keep "expert" only where the field truly requires a human reviewer or specialist
label. For general model outputs, prefer neutral terms such as "review",
"annotation", "label", "match", or "score".
